OGS family game · concept sketch

Bobberbrook

A cozy 3D fishing world for the whole couch. Each of you runs your own little adventurer through a hand-painted land, finds the sparkling fishing spots, and brings the catch home to one shared trophy wall. No combat, no losing, no rush.

The feeling

Classic fantasy-MMO wandering, shrunk to a family-sized world

The WoW part is the sense of place: chunky hand-painted zones with their own weather and music, a hub town you return to, a fishing journal to fill, gear that gets visibly fancier, and a rare glowing fish everyone talks about. The cozy part is that nothing can go wrong. A missed bite is just a splash and a giggle, and a 3-year-old catches fish too.

Group camera that frames all four players, zooms in for big catches, and shows the trophy wall back in town. Nothing ever covers the middle.

Juneau's iPad

His own adventurer

Over-the-shoulder view. Left thumb walks, right thumb casts and reels. Picture icons for his bag and journal, no reading needed.

Little sister's iPad

Buddy mode

Her character follows a grown-up automatically. One big button: tap to cast, tap the splash to catch. She can't miss, and her fish count like everyone's.

Grown-ups' phones

Same game, smaller

Full controls, plus the "lead" role: pick where the family goes next, choose the campfire recipe, end the session.

The 20-second loop

Cast, wait, splash, catch

  1. Find a spotSparkling swirls of fish drift around each zone, like WoW's fishing pools.
  2. CastHold to aim the arc, let go. The bobber plops in with a satisfying sound.
  3. Wait for the dipLittle nibbles, then a big splash. Tap on the splash.
  4. ReelA short tug-of-war: hold when the fish tires, ease off when it pulls. Toddler mode skips it.
  5. The revealThe fish leaps out, the TV cuts in close, its name is spoken aloud.
  6. Journal itNew fish fill a page in the family journal. Old boots and teapots happen too, for giggles.
Places to fish

Five zones, each with its own trick

Every zone changes how fishing works a little, so the loop stays fresh. New zones open as the family journal fills, never by grinding levels.

Before we build

Decisions for you

Where the 3D renders.Recommendation: each iPad and phone renders its own Three.js view of a world synced through one Durable Object, and the TV page renders the group camera on the cast stream. The cheaper option is TV-only 3D with devices as controllers, which is less like running around in WoW.
Free roam or zone hops.Recommendation: each zone is one small open map you run around freely, and you travel between zones from the harbor dock. Keeps the TV camera able to frame four players.
The name.Bobberbrook, Lantern Lake or Tidewhistle.
First playable.Recommendation: Coral Lagoon alone, the cast and splash loop, 10 fish, the journal, toddler buddy mode, on the TV and both iPads. Everything else after the kids have played it.
