# AAA scorecard: Bobberbrook (graded like a first-party Nintendo / AAA studio review)

**Audience: family.** Dad on a phone, Juneau (5) on an iPad, his sister (almost 3) on an iPad in easy
mode, Mom on a phone, everyone watching one TV. Kid rows apply.

References (the "10"): Animal Crossing: New Horizons fishing, Stardew Valley fishing (loop and
collection), World of Warcraft (Elwynn Forest / Nagrand painted look, fishing pools, the bobber
splash), A Short Hike, Dredge's catch reveal, Super Mario Party / Mario Kart couch UI. A 7 = "competent
indie demo". **An 8 = a first-party art director would sign off shipping it.** Grade every row
against the references, never against the previous round. Stop when every row ≥ 8 (after ≥ 3 rounds).

| Row | 6 | 8 (ship bar) | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Art direction & cohesion | consistent-ish, generic low-poly | one unmistakable hand-painted fantasy-MMO style across TV, controllers, journal and catch cards; strong shape language; palette with intent | instantly recognisable key art |
| Environment & set dressing | a lake with props on it | a place with identity: the dock, lily pads, waterfall cliff, reeds and village read from across the room; dense, layered, lush vegetation; no empty floor | Elwynn-grade "I want to live there" |
| Water & fishing FX | flat plane, a sphere bobber | painted water with depth, shore foam, reflections; the bobber plop, nibble dips, the BITE splash and reel thrash are crisp and satisfying | water you could watch for minutes |
| Characters & animation | capsules that slide | appealing chunky fishers with personality, walk/idle/cast/reel/hold-up poses, anticipation and follow-through, never sliding or snapping | characters you'd buy as toys |
| Lighting, time of day & mood | even daylight | morning, golden hour, night and dawn each beautiful; warm key, cool fill, soft shadows; night glows (lanterns, fireflies, glowing fish) | every frame is a painting |
| Camera & composition | wanders, crops fishers | every fisher always framed and findable, eased group camera, a satisfying push-in on catches, the focal area clear of HUD | key-art frames |
| Catch moment & payoff | a fish appears | the fish leaps, is held up, the TV, the catcher's device and the narrator celebrate together; new species feel special; rarities read | you cheer every time |
| Game feel & responsiveness | presses register | every press answers instantly on the device; casting, hooking and the reel tug-of-war feel tactile; walking feels good despite stream latency | Nintendo-grade feel |
| Kid & little-kid UX | usable with help | a 5-year-old plays with no words: he always knows where he is, when to tap and when to hold; a 3-year-old in easy mode can't fail | effortless |
| Grown-up phone UI | functional | first-party UI: the coach line makes Dad talk to Juneau, journal and options are clear, no dev feel | invisible |
| Progression & variety | catches repeat | the journal, rods, swirls, golden swirls, time-of-day fish and the Moonfin give real reasons to keep going and come back | kids ask for tomorrow |
| Audio | pleasant synth | a cozy soundtrack and foley (water, birds, crickets, splash, reel) that fit the place, mixed, never harsh; the narrator is warm and well-timed | a soundtrack you'd hum |
| Onboarding & legibility | the rules need explaining | a first-time player learns by playing and can say what caused every outcome on screen | understood from across the room |
| Performance & stability | ok | 1080p p95 frame ≤ 16.7 ms on a GPU, 30 fps on the stream, shader programs flat, no hitches, no black frames, no glitches | — |
