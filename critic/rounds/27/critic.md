## Largest actionable gap

**Mistmarsh catch framing.** The catch push-in flies through near-camera wisteria curtains, which wash over the catcher (121 s, 129 s). That breaks the "nothing covers the focal area" veto. Meanwhile the two fishers stand shoulder to shoulder at the dock foot, one of them in the water. This one place drags Camera to 4, Catch and Chars down, and it is the moment every Mistmarsh round ends on.

## Top 8 fixes (ranked by score impact per effort)

1. **[scene] Fade near-camera occluders along the camera path, not just at rest.** Every frame, raycast or frustum-test from the camera to each fisher's head, chest and held-fish points. Any scenery mesh (wisteria strands, willow cards, canopy) inside that cone or within about 4 m of the near plane dithers out over 150 ms. Fixes 120.9–121.1 s and 128.6–129.2 s. **[rig]** Run the scenery-over-fisher check on every frame of the catch push-in (0–1.4 s), not only the settled still. Today mistmarsh-catch passes at cover 0 while the session shows a full-frame curtain.
2. **[rules] Mistmarsh fishing slots.** Spread the slots at least 3 m apart along the shore, all on dry land and not at the dock foot. Juneau stands in the shallows tucked behind Dad for 115–146 s (mistmarsh-dock-foot, 121 s). Add a lab assert that "fisher foot point is on land" and that the screen-space gap between catchers is at least 1 body width.
3. **[rules] Trail stall, 50–92 s.** Dad's stick is held down-left and his position doesn't change for 42 s. Find the collision snag (the white rock or bird by his feet, or the trail edge), make movement slide along blockers, and add a small bump wobble on the TV and a buzz or icon shake on the device. **[rig]** Flag "input held ≥ 2 s with < 0.2 m displacement".
4. **[ui] Kid trail cue.** Keep the iPad direction arrow up for the whole trail (it vanishes about 55–90 s), and drop the "go to water" badge from the cast button when the nearest water is more than 10 m away (strip-ipad 1-walk, 55–90 s). **[ui]** When the room's progress stalls for more than 15 s, swap the coach line to something actionable ("Walk Juneau along the path toward the fog").
5. **[audio] Mistmarsh foley and sting mix.** Mistmarsh casts, plops, bite splashes and hooks are silent (0 onsets in 105–118 s and 133–142 s). Juneau's treasure chest (143 s) needs its own soft reveal. Low-pass or soften the catch-sting transients above 3 kHz (38 harsh/min at 144 s, 29 at 7 s). Add trail foley: footsteps on soil, birds, a distant stream under the 74 s pad.
6. **[rules] New place, new fish.** Weight each place's first catches toward species not yet in the journal. Pond Pike repeats at 121 s and 129 s, and the journal stays 4/30 across 4 Mistmarsh catches, which contradicts the coach line on screen.
7. **[scene] Fisher materials and fog.** Use matte cloth and skin (roughness ≥ 0.85, no clearcoat, painted AO in the folds) and a hair shape and colour for Mom to match 08-characters (she reads bald and bleached at the campfire). Exclude fishers from the fog sheets and the dawn haze (moonwell_tod-dawn: Mom ghosted). Push the catch close-up to concept-face quality (mistmarsh-catch looks like a vinyl toy).
8. **[scene] Mistmarsh frame cost and wide framing.** Bring Mistmarsh's p95 33.4 ms (all four times of day) to 16.7 ms by instancing and LOD-ing the wisteria strands and lily pads and cutting their overdraw. Raise the group camera's minimum fisher height (four-reel: about 5% of frame, Juneau and Mom stacked) so each fisher is at least about 9% of frame height on the TV.
